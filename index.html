Intransitive & Transitive Verbs

الفِعْلُ اللَّازِمُ وَالفِعْلُ المُتَعَدِّي

اللَّازِمُ وَالمُتَعَدِّي
Does the Action Stop, or Pass On?

Some verbs are complete with just a doer — جَلَسَ إِبْرَاهِيمُ — and some need something to act on — قَرَأَ إِبْرَاهِيمُ كِتَابَهُ. Learn to tell them apart, and the three ways Arabic turns the first kind into the second, with verses from the Qur’an.

لازم — stays متعدٍّ — passes on
The big idea

يَقِفُ الفِعْلُ أَمْ يَتَعَدَّى؟The action stays with the doer — or reaches an object

لَازِم
Intransitive
جَلَسَ إِبْرَاهِيمُ
doer ⟲
Ibrahim sat. Nothing receives the action.
مُتَعَدٍّ
Transitive
قَرَأَ إِبْرَاهِيمُ كِتَابَهُ
doer ➜ object
Ibrahim read his book. The action lands on something.
verb doer (subject) object
i
The thing that receives the action is the object (المَفْعُولُ بِهِ), and it takes the accusative ending — كِتَابَهُ ends in fatḥa.
Intransitive · لَازِم

الفِعْلُ اللَّازِمComplete with just a doer

جَلَسَ إِبْرَاهِيمُIbrahim sat
خَفَّتْ مَوَازِينُهُhis scales are light
جَلَسَ إِبْرَاهِيمُ عَلَى الكُرْسِيِّIbrahim sat on the chair
ذَهَبَ عُمَرُ إِلَى السُّوقِUmar went to the market
verb doer preposition + noun
!
The amber phrases (عَلَى الكُرْسِيِّ، إِلَى السُّوقِ) do not make the verb transitive. An intransitive verb reaches a noun only through a preposition, and the noun after it is genitive, not accusative.
From the Qur’an
وَمَنْ خَفَّتْ مَوَازِينُهُ فَأُولَٰئِكَ الَّذِينَ خَسِرُوا أَنفُسَهُمْ
Sūrah al-Mu’minūn, 23:103 (also 7:9) — “and those whose scales are light are the ones who have lost themselves.” This is the deck’s own example.
وَذَا النُّونِ إِذ ذَّهَبَ مُغَاضِبًا
Sūrah al-Anbiyā’, 21:87 — “and [remember] Dhun-Nun, when he went off in anger.” No object.
فَخَرَجَ مِنْهَا خَائِفًا يَتَرَقَّبُ
Sūrah al-Qaṣaṣ, 28:21 — “so he left it, fearful and watchful.” مِنْهَا is a preposition + pronoun (genitive).
فَسَجَدُوا إِلَّا إِبْلِيسَ
Sūrah al-Baqarah, 2:34 — “so they prostrated, except Iblīs.”
اذْهَبْ إِلَىٰ فِرْعَوْنَ إِنَّهُ طَغَىٰ
Sūrah Ṭā-Hā, 20:24 — “Go to Pharaoh, for he has transgressed.” The same verb ذَهَبَ + إِلَى as the deck’s Umar sentence. (فِرْعَوْنَ is genitive here, but looks like fatḥa because it is a diptote.)
Transitive · مُتَعَدٍّ

الفِعْلُ المُتَعَدِّيThe action lands on an object

قَرَأَ إِبْرَاهِيمُ كِتَابَهُIbrahim read his book
قَتَلَ دَاوُودُ جَالُوتَDavid killed Goliath
أَكَلَ زَيْدٌ الرَّغِيفَZayd ate the loaf
verb doer object
i
Look at the object endings: all accusative. جَالُوتَ is a foreign name — a diptote — so it takes fatḥa with no tanwīn, exactly as in your Diptotes lesson.
From the Qur’an
وَقَتَلَ دَاوُودُ جَالُوتَ
Sūrah al-Baqarah, 2:251 — “and David killed Goliath.” The deck’s own example.
إِنَّا ذَهَبْنَا نَسْتَبِقُ وَتَرَكْنَا يُوسُفَ عِندَ مَتَاعِنَا فَأَكَلَهُ الذِّئْبُ
Sūrah Yūsuf, 12:17 — “We went racing and left Joseph with our things, and the wolf ate him.” One verse shows all three: ذَهَبْنَا is intransitive, while تَرَكْنَا يُوسُفَ and أَكَلَهُ are transitive.
كَتَبَ رَبُّكُمْ عَلَىٰ نَفْسِهِ الرَّحْمَةَ
Sūrah al-An‘ām, 6:54 — “Your Lord has decreed mercy upon Himself.”
فَإِذَا قَرَأْنَاهُ فَاتَّبِعْ قُرْآنَهُ
Sūrah al-Qiyāmah, 75:18 — “So when We have recited it, follow its recitation.” The object is the attached pronoun ـهُ.
خَلَقَ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضَ بِالْحَقِّ
Sūrah an-Naḥl, 16:3 — “He created the heavens and the earth in truth.”
Two quick tests

كَيْفَ أُمَيِّزُ بَيْنَهُمَا؟How to tell them apart

Test 1 — ask “what?” or “whom?” قَرَأَ → what did he read? → كِتَابَهُ ✓ transitive.   جَلَسَ → sat what? → no answer ✗ intransitive.
Test 2 — try an attached pronoun A transitive verb takes the object pronoun straight onto its end (you practised this with كَتَبَهَا). An intransitive verb cannot. Tap each card.

i

From the Qur’an — one root, two ways
نَزَّلَ عَلَيْكَ الْكِتَابَ بِالْحَقِّ مُصَدِّقًا لِّمَا بَيْنَ يَدَيْهِ وَأَنزَلَ التَّوْرَاةَ وَالْإِنجِيلَ
Sūrah Āl ‘Imrān, 3:3 — “He has sent down to you the Book in truth, confirming what came before it, and He sent down the Torah and the Gospel.” The intransitive نَزَلَ (to descend) becomes transitive both by doubling (نَزَّلَ, Form II) and by the hamza (أَنزَلَ, Form IV).
Way 1 · Form IV

هَمْزَةُ النَّقْل — أَفْعَلَA hamza carries the action to an object

The ideaThe doer of the old verb becomes the one affected: “the boy stood” → “the mother made the boy stand.” Tap each card.
قَامَ الصَّبِيُّ
↓
أَقَامَتِ الأُمُّ الصَّبِيَّ
the boy stood → the mother stood the boy up. (The boy has gone from subject, ـُ, to object, ـَ.)
tap ▸
خَرَجَ مُحَمَّدٌ
↓
أَخْرَجَ اللهُ الحَيَّ مِنَ المَيِّتِ
Muhammad went out → Allah brought the living out of the dead
tap ▸
From the Qur’an
إِنَّ الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا وَعَمِلُوا الصَّالِحَاتِ وَأَقَامُوا الصَّلَاةَ
Sūrah al-Baqarah, 2:277 — “those who believe, do good deeds and establish prayer.” قَامَ (to stand) → أَقَامَ (to establish), with the object الصَّلَاةَ.
فَأَخْرَجَ بِهِ مِنَ الثَّمَرَاتِ رِزْقًا لَّكُمْ
Sūrah al-Baqarah, 2:22 — “and brought forth fruits thereby as provision for you.” خَرَجَ → أَخْرَجَ.
يُخْرِجُ الْحَيَّ مِنَ الْمَيِّتِ
Sūrah al-An‘ām, 6:95 — “He brings the living out of the dead.” The present tense of the deck’s own example, أَخْرَجَ.
Way 2 · Form II

تَضْعِيفُ العَيْنِ — فَعَّلَDoubling the middle letter

The ideaDoubling the middle root letter (the ع of فَعَلَ) makes the verb act on something — often as “make X do it” or “do it intensively.” Tap each card.
ذَهَبَ مُحَمَّدٌ
↓
ذَهَّبَ يُوسُفُ الحَدِيدَ
Muhammad went → Yusuf gilded the iron
tap ▸
عَلِمَ المُعَلِّمُ
↓
عَلَّمَ المُعَلِّمُ الطَّالِبَ
the teacher knew → the teacher taught the student
tap ▸
i
عَلِمَ is already transitive (“knew something”). Doubling it gives “taught”, which can take two objects: the person and the thing taught. Some verbs naturally take two objects, as in the verses below.
From the Qur’an
عَلَّمَ الْإِنسَانَ مَا لَمْ يَعْلَمْ
Sūrah al-‘Alaq, 96:5 — “He taught man what he did not know.” عَلَّمَ (Form II) and يَعْلَمْ (the simple verb) side by side; two objects: الْإِنسَانَ and مَا.
فَفَهَّمْنَاهَا سُلَيْمَانَ
Sūrah al-Anbiyā’, 21:79 — “and We made Solomon understand it.” فَهِمَ (to understand) → فَهَّمَ (to make understand), with two objects: ـهَا and سُلَيْمَانَ.
إِنَّا أَعْطَيْنَاكَ الْكَوْثَرَ
Sūrah al-Kawthar, 108:1 — “Indeed We have given you al-Kawthar.” Another two-object verb: ـكَ and الْكَوْثَرَ.
Way 3 · Form III

أَلِفُ المُشَارَكَة — فَاعَلَAn alif that brings in a partner

The ideaThe alif means doing the action with or against someone. That partner becomes the object. Tap each card.
لَعِبَ زَيْدٌ
↓
لَاعَبَ زَيْدٌ عَلِيًّا
Zayd played → Zayd played with Ali
tap ▸
جَلَسَ الوَلَدُ
↓
جَالَسَ الوَلَدُ أُمَّهُ
the boy sat → the boy sat with his mother
tap ▸
From the Qur’an — Form III verbs with their objects
قَدْ جَادَلْتَنَا فَأَكْثَرْتَ جِدَالَنَا
Sūrah Hūd, 11:32 — “You have disputed with us and been excessive in disputing.” The object is نَا.
الَّذِينَ يُقَاتِلُونَكُمْ
Sūrah al-Baqarah, 2:190 — “those who fight you.” The object is كُمْ.
جَاهِدِ الْكُفَّارَ وَالْمُنَافِقِينَ
Sūrah at-Tawbah, 9:73 — “Strive against the disbelievers and the hypocrites.” Two objects, both accusative.
i
These Qur’anic verbs show the job of the alif. I am not claiming each one comes from an intransitive verb.
